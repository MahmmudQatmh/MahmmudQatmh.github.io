Featured project · University of Turku, 2026

BlueSim: a multi-robot simulator with ROS 2

Testing a new controller on a real boat costs a field day and carries risk. I built BlueSim in Unreal Engine 5.4.4 so our lab could test perception and control on a surface boat, a drone and a ground rover in one shared world first. Each robot has physics-based motion and an onboard camera, and all three are controlled over ROS 2 through the rclUE bridge.

Showcase: the environment and the three robots.

How it fits together

  1. Unreal Engine 5Physics for boat, drone and rover · onboard cameras (640×360, 90° FOV)
  2. rclUE bridgeC++ publishers and subscribers inside the simulator
  3. ROS 2 topics/camera_BlueBoat · /cmd_BlueBoat_vel · pan/tilt camera control
  4. Python nodesAutonomy, experiments and camera monitoring, sending Twist commands back

Engineering choices

  • Robot physics is kept separate from the command source, so the same robot runs from the keyboard or from ROS 2 code without changes.
  • Standard ROS 2 messages (Twist, Image), so lab code can later move to the real BlueBoat.
  • Built-in experiment for camera resolution against publish rate (5–30 Hz), to find the bandwidth limits before field tests.
  • Next: LiDAR (in progress), then IMU, GPS, sonar and odometry.

Research Assistant · University of Turku · Summer 2026

Building a survey boat for real water

The department needed a working uncrewed surface vessel for sensing research. I assembled and configured a BlueRobotics BlueBoat from the hardware up, integrated its sensors, set up the ROS 2 environment and supported field testing.

  • Autopilot & compute: Raspberry Pi 4, ArduPilot, BlueOS
  • Sensors: ExploreHD camera, Cerulean Surveyor 240-16 multibeam echosounder, Cerulean OmniScan 450 side-scan sonar
  • Software: ROS 2 Jazzy environment, with measurements of network performance and communication latency for sensor streams
  • Result: a field-tested platform for the lab, plus BlueSim as its digital twin for testing code before going on the water

Kongsberg Maritime Middle East · Dubai · 2023–2025

Field work: dynamic positioning on real vessels

Dynamic positioning (DP) keeps a vessel on an exact spot or track using its thrusters, sensors and a redundant control system, with no anchor. It is an early, safety-critical form of marine autonomy. As a DP service engineer I worked on these systems on board, during commissioning, upgrades and breakdowns.
