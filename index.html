Mass-aware collision avoidance for small USVs in harbours

Small uncrewed surface vessels working in harbours have to pass buoys and posts at close range, but they can only carry light, low-cost sensors. My thesis builds a collision-avoidance method for a catamaran USV that uses only a monocular camera, a 2D LiDAR and a safety filter based on High-Order Control Barrier Functions (HOCBF).

The key idea is that the safety filter is written directly in thrust force and yaw moment, so the boat's mass, moment of inertia and hydrodynamic drag enter every constraint. A filter that ignores mass assumes the boat can stop or turn faster than it physically can.

  1. Camera depthDepth Anything 3 gives relative depth from one camera. LiDAR returns that the camera also sees convert it to metres.
  2. LiDAR map with memoryA world-fixed obstacle map keeps obstacles in sectors the camera can't see.
  3. HOCBF safety filterA quadratic program minimally changes the thrust and yaw moment commands, using a surge-yaw model with mass, inertia and drag.
  4. Seven-disc hull coverThe hull is covered by seven discs, so the filter can use yaw to swing the bow away from an obstacle.
  5. Stopping-distance speed limitSpeed is capped from the vessel's stopping distance, so the QP stays feasible under thrust limits.
  6. ROS 2 + BlueSimImplemented in ROS 2 and evaluated in BlueSim, my Unreal Engine 5 simulator, with floating buoys.

Featured project · University of Turku, 2026

BlueSim: a multi-robot simulator with ROS 2

Testing a new controller on a real boat costs a field day and carries risk. I built BlueSim in Unreal Engine 5.4.4 so our lab could test perception and control on a surface boat, a drone and a ground rover in one shared world first. Each robot has physics-based motion and an onboard camera, and all three are controlled over ROS 2 through the rclUE bridge.

Showcase: the environment and the three robots.

How it fits together

  1. Unreal Engine 5Physics for boat, drone and rover · onboard cameras (640×360, 90° FOV)
  2. rclUE bridgeC++ publishers and subscribers inside the simulator
  3. ROS 2 topics/camera_BlueBoat · /cmd_BlueBoat_vel · pan/tilt camera control
  4. Python nodesAutonomy, experiments and camera monitoring, sending Twist commands back

Engineering choices

  • Robot physics is kept separate from the command source, so the same robot runs from the keyboard or from ROS 2 code without changes.
  • Standard ROS 2 messages (Twist, Image), so lab code can later move to the real BlueBoat.
  • Built-in experiment for camera resolution against publish rate (5–30 Hz), to find the bandwidth limits before field tests.
  • Floating buoys and a 2D LiDAR are now used for my thesis on USV collision avoidance. Next: IMU, GPS, sonar and odometry.

Field work: dynamic positioning on real vessels

Dynamic positioning (DP) keeps a ship on an exact spot or heading using only its own thrusters, with no anchor. Offshore supply, drilling, construction and survey vessels rely on it to work next to platforms and over subsea wells in wind, waves and current. It is one of the oldest forms of marine autonomy that is safety-critical. As a DP service engineer at Kongsberg, I commissioned, tested and repaired these systems on board.

Why so many sensors?

A DP system never trusts a single measurement. It compares several independent position references and rejects any that drift or freeze. It blends them with heading, motion and wind data in a model of the vessel, so the ship holds position even when a sensor fails.

The control loop, many times per second

  1. MeasurePosition references, gyro, MRU, wind
  2. Check & voteReject frozen, jumping or out-of-range signals
  3. EstimateVessel model + Kalman filter; filters out wave motion
  4. ControlPosition & heading control plus wind feed-forward
  5. AllocateSplit the force across tunnel and azimuth thrusters
  6. ActThrusters push; the vessel holds position

Redundancy is set by DP class: class 2 and 3 vessels must keep position after any single failure. Class 3 must also survive a fire or flood in one compartment. That's why everything comes in pairs or threes, and why commissioning and failure-mode testing take so long.
