Mass-aware collision avoidance for small USVs in harbours

Camera + 2D LiDAR perception and a High-Order Control Barrier Function safety filter written in thrust and yaw moment, so mass, inertia and drag enter every constraint.

Year
2026
Type
M.Sc. thesis
Platform
Catamaran USV
Evaluation

The problem

Small uncrewed surface vessels working in harbours have to pass buoys and posts at close range, but they can only carry light, low-cost sensors. A collision-avoidance method for them has to work with one camera and a 2D LiDAR, and it has to respect what the boat can physically do: a heavy boat can't stop or turn as fast as a light one.

Approach

  1. Metric depth from one cameraDepth Anything 3 gives relative depth. LiDAR returns that the camera also sees convert it to metres.
  2. LiDAR map with memoryA world-fixed obstacle map keeps obstacles in sectors the camera can't see.
  3. HOCBF safety filter in force and momentThe filter is written directly in thrust force and yaw moment on a surge-yaw model, so mass, moment of inertia and hydrodynamic drag enter every constraint.
  4. Seven-disc hull coverThe hull is covered by seven discs, so the filter can use yaw to swing the bow away from an obstacle.
  5. Stopping-distance speed limitSpeed is capped from the vessel's stopping distance, which keeps the quadratic program feasible under thrust limits.
  6. ROS 2 + BlueSimImplemented in ROS 2 and evaluated in BlueSim, my Unreal Engine 5 simulator, with floating buoys.

The interactive simulation above is a simplified version of the idea running in your browser. Switch between the mass-aware filter and an acceleration-based filter that ignores mass.

Results

Evaluation runs in BlueSim are being finalised. The paper compares the mass-aware filter with an acceleration-based HOCBF that ignores the vessel's mass.
