Underwater fleet coordination for synthetic aperture sonar

Four robots hold a rigid formation as a "virtual sonar array" while a distributed QP-CBF filter avoids LiDAR-detected obstacles.

The scenario

High-resolution synthetic aperture sonar (SAS) needs a large physical array. Instead of one large sensor, four robots act as a virtual array: they keep a precise, symmetric 90° formation while moving through an unknown environment. Four TurtleBot 4s stand in for underwater ROVs.

Technical approach

  • Decentralised estimation: every robot broadcasts its state at 50 Hz over a custom ROS 2 StateExchange message. There is no leader: each robot computes the fleet centre itself and holds its slot.
  • Safety-critical control: nominal formation velocities pass through a Quadratic Program with Control Barrier Functions, solved with OSQP, so the robots avoid obstacles without breaking the formation.
  • LiDAR pipeline: laser scans are projected to world coordinates, filtered to 0.1–3.0 m, and the 10 closest points become hard constraints in the QP.

Lessons learned

  • Tuning the look-ahead distance of the feedback linearisation was critical for stability.
  • Switching to sparse (CSC) matrices fixed ROS 2 loop-rate lag under dense LiDAR input.
